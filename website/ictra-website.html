The seizures nobody sees are the ones that kill. ICTRA sees them.

ICTRA is a low-cost wearable that reads brain waves (EEG), heart rhythm (ECG) and muscle activity (EMG) at once, fuses them with a trained multimodal AI, and wakes a caregiver within seconds of seizure onset. Built by four high-school students on open hardware and public clinical data.

Rubric · Problem Statement · national and local importance, impact on multiple fronts

Between hospital visits, families are alone with the risk.

Epilepsy is one of the most common neurological diseases in the world, yet the moment that matters most, the seizure itself, usually happens with no one watching and no record kept. The gap has four fronts.

Safety

Unwitnessed convulsions

Most SUDEP deaths (sudden unexpected death in epilepsy) follow an unwitnessed convulsive seizure, usually at night. Nocturnal supervision is a known protective factor. Langan 2005 ↗

Clinical care

Wrong seizure counts

Medication is adjusted on patient diaries that miss more than half of all seizures and almost all seizures during sleep. Hoppe 2007 ↗

Access

Monitoring needs a hospital

Video-EEG needs a bed and specialists. Routine EEG lasts under an hour; capturing epileptic spasms in 95% of patients took up to 19 extra hours of monitoring. In BC, some families wait over a year.

Trust

Devices that cry wolf

Consumer seizure watches sense motion or sweat, so they catch only convulsive seizures and raise frequent false alarms. Caregivers switch them off.

The question ICTRA answers

Can a student-buildable wearable that fuses EEG, ECG and EMG with a trained multimodal model detect seizures with clinically useful sensitivity and fewer than one false alarm per day, on patients it has never seen?

Rubric · AI + Originality · leveraging AI's unique abilities

A seizure is a brain event with body-wide consequences. So we listen to three organs.

Within seconds of onset, the EEG shows rhythmic discharges, the heart rate jumps rather than ramps, and muscles fire in sustained high-frequency bursts. No single signal is reliable on its own; together they are hard to fake.

Before any classifier: we measure the order of events

Our first experiment is not a detector. For every annotated seizure in the development set we align brain, heart and muscle to the onset and measure which signal moves first and by how many seconds: lag_eeg_to_hr, lag_eeg_to_emg, lag_emg_to_acc, by seizure type. The same question a landslide study asks of rain, soil water and ground motion. Heart and muscle are only useful at the moments they actually respond, so this timeline decides which signal to trust for which seizure. The measured version of the schematic above lands in Results.

Rubric · AI + Technical Skills

How a seizure travels through ICTRA

Three sensors, three specialist models, one fused decision. Only the fused and temporally filtered probability can trigger an alert, which is what keeps false alarms down.

SenseBehind-the-ear or forehead EEG electrodes, a chest or arm ECG lead and a biceps EMG pair feed low-cost analog front-end modules wired to an Arduino UNO R3.
AcquireBioSignal Studio firmware streams each channel over serial at 115,200 baud. The current firmware samples EEG at 256 Hz, ECG at 125 Hz and EMG at 500 Hz; the training pipeline resamples everything to 256 Hz, and the next firmware revision will sample at 256 Hz directly so the device and the benchmark share one code path.
WindowEvery stream is cut into 4-second windows with a 2-second hop on one shared clock. Heart rate is also compared with its own rolling 5-minute baseline, so the model sees a jump, not an absolute number.
RecogniseGradient-boosted trees read EEG band powers and line length for ictal rhythms, heart-rate and variability features for ictal tachycardia, and EMG envelope and movement features for tonic and clonic activation. Trees train in minutes, run on a laptop, and their feature importances can be read by a clinician.
FuseModel C sees all signals at once (early fusion). A late-fusion variant trains one model per signal and averages their probabilities, re-weighting when a signal is flat or missing; the development folds decide which one is frozen.
DecideA k-of-n rule (3 of the last 5 windows above τ) suppresses isolated spikes before anything fires, trading a few seconds of latency for far fewer false alarms. τ is tuned on validation patients only and frozen before the test set is opened.
Alert and reviewThe caregiver's phone gets a push alert with the time and a 60-second signal snapshot; the clinician dashboard logs every event for confirm/reject and export.
Rubric · Artificial Intelligence /25 · the Five Big Ideas

Why this problem needs AI, and where the Five Big Ideas live in ICTRA

A heart-rate threshold alarms on a staircase. An EMG threshold alarms on a shopping bag. An EEG amplitude threshold alarms on every blink. What identifies a seizure is the joint, time-evolving pattern across brain, heart and muscle, and it differs by person and seizure type. Learning that pattern from hundreds of annotated seizures is what a model can do and a hand-written rule cannot.

BIG IDEA 1

Perception

Electrodes turn microvolt activity into samples; filters and spectrograms turn samples into features the model can see. We keep 1–25 Hz for EEG because ictal rhythms live there, and we filter out mains hum rather than learn it.

BIG IDEA 2

Representation & Reasoning

A 4 s window becomes a short vector of physical quantities: band powers and line length for the brain, heart rate and its rise over the last five minutes for the heart, burst energy for the muscle. The model reasons over those, and we can read back which ones it used.

BIG IDEA 3

Learning

Supervised learning from clinician-annotated seizures in SeizeIT2 only, split by patient. The locked test patients and five external datasets are opened once, after a hashed pre-registration. Within-patient splits inflate accuracy; we refuse them.

BIG IDEA 4

Natural Interaction

The system talks to humans at the right moment: an alert a caregiver can act on, a dashboard a neurologist can audit, no screen the patient must watch.

BIG IDEA 5

Societal Impact

A medical-grade capability at consumer cost, data kept local, and an ethics section that names who the model was not trained on.

Rubric · Technical Skills · mastery of software development and execution

126 gigabytes, 11,626 hours, and one page that watches all of it

A seizure is under one percent of the recording time, so the only way to measure false alarms honestly is to process every hour. That made the data engineering the first real engineering problem of the project, before any model was trained.

126 GBSeizeIT2 in full: 11,009 signal files, each verified against its SHA-256 before it is used
95 GBdevelopment set downloaded for training: every seizure hour first, then every seizure-free hour
2,850recording runs, up to 18 hours each, catalogued before a single byte of signal was downloaded
~3,800×real time: feature extraction streams each run in one-hour chunks so a 3 GB laptop copes
30 srefresh of the ICTRA Lab page that watches downloads, features and training jobs
Download

Resumable, verified, parallel

A catalog of every file with its size and checksum is built from the dataset's metadata first. The downloader then streams files to disk, resumes interrupted transfers, verifies each one, and lets several processes share the work through per-file locks that record their owner, so a crashed process never corrupts a file. Locked-test patients are refused until the pre-registration hash exists.

Features

One code path, every signal

Every stream is resampled to 256 Hz, filtered, cut into 4-second windows and turned into physical features by a single library that the live device will also run. Flat or saturated stretches (one ECG file sat at a constant value for eight hours) are flagged and blanked rather than learned as "normal". A change to that library forces every feature to be rebuilt, by rule.

Monitor

Scripts write numbers, the page only draws

The ICTRA Lab page shows download bars, files in flight with their speed, feature counters, the training-job grid, disk space and the log, and it refreshes itself. It never computes a metric: every script writes JSON and the page reads it, so the numbers on screen are the numbers in the report. A stall watch restarts any download that goes quiet for five minutes.

Screenshots taken on 17 October 2026 during the first download. The page runs locally on the training machine; a frozen copy is published with the results.

Rubric · Technical Skills · the hardware exists and is verified

BioSignal Studio: the open acquisition platform behind ICTRA

An Arduino UNO R3 acquisition stack with selectable EEG, ECG and EMG modes, live waveform preview and CSV/JSON/PNG export. Firmware checksums are pinned; a hardware verification report (2026-10-03) covers transport, recording and export. Public on GitHub ↗.

What is in the box

Arduino UNO R3 boards (one per modality in the current prototype), EEG/ECG/EMG analog front-end modules, gel snap and dry electrodes, USB cables. All modes use the UNO's A0 input at 115,200 baud. Measurements are ADC counts; microvolt calibration is a listed next step, which is why the models train on per-recording normalised signals.

Bill of materials: cost TBD · target: buildable from the repository in one afternoon.

Safety by design

Body acquisition only with the laptop on battery and no mains peripherals (the README rule). Firmware uploads verify source and HEX checksums in profiles.json. Lead-off detection marks data as missing rather than normal, so a fallen electrode degrades sensitivity visibly, never silently.

Next revision

All channels at 256 Hz with the training pipeline's filters, an accelerometer (SeizeIT2 has one and it is the cheapest strong signal for convulsions), then a single ESP32-class board with a three-channel front end, Bluetooth to a phone and on-device inference for the tree models. Honest status: the current board still saturates on part of its range, so model results come from SeizeIT2 and the board demonstrates feasibility.

Rubric · Design /10 · visual and auditory design, user experience

Three users with opposite needs

The patient wants to forget the device exists. The caregiver wants to be woken only when it matters. The clinician wants evidence, not a diary. ICTRA gives each a different surface.

Patient

Nothing to manage

Discreet behind-the-ear and under-sleeve electrodes, no on-body display, one button to start and stop, electrodes changed daily. The patient sees every alert sent about them and controls the device; there is no location tracking.

Caregiver

Woken reliably, told how serious

Two-stage tone: a soft chime for "possible", a continuous alarm for "confirmed". Elapsed time counts up. One tap, "I'm with them", silences the alarm and logs the response time. Night mode uses a 1–3 kHz band and a paired-watch vibration.

Clinician

Evidence behind every entry

A timeline of alerts with the three raw traces around each event, confirm/reject buttons, and CSV/PDF export for the appointment. Rejections feed the next training round.

One colour per signal, everywhere

EEG, ECG and EMG keep the same colour on the live waveform screen, in the alert snapshot and on the dashboard, so nobody re-learns the mapping. Colour is never the only cue: each state also has an icon and a word.

One number for the caregiver

The fused probability is the only quantity a caregiver sees, as a single rising bar. Per-signal detail lives one tap deeper, for the clinician.

Words that match what the system knows

"Possible seizure started 00:12 ago", never "Seizure detected". Setup is a four-screen wizard with electrode-placement photos, because placement is the step most likely to go wrong.

Rubric · AI Ethics /10 · from design to implementation, including unintended consequences

The two errors are not symmetric, and neither is our design

We treated ethics as design constraints. Each concern below names the decision it produced.

False negative · missed seizure

A caregiver trusts the device and is not woken

Worst case
SUDEP after an unwitnessed convulsive seizure
Response
Operating point tuned for sensitivity first; per-seizure-type sensitivity published so users know where ICTRA is weak; first-launch screen states it is a monitoring aid, not a guarantee
False positive · false alarm

Alarm fatigue turns into muted devices

Worst case
The caregiver mutes it, converting every future alert into a false negative
Response
Multimodal fusion plus the k-of-n rule; two-stage alarm; a two-tap "false alarm" mark that feeds retraining

Not a medical device

ICTRA is a student research prototype, not approved by Health Canada, the FDA or any regulator. It must not change medication or replace prescribed monitoring. The pathway to a real product runs through prospective clinical validation and a Class II software-as-a-medical-device submission, described in Impact rather than pretended done.

Privacy: brain data stays home

Training data is public and de-identified. Live data stays on the user's machine (BioSignal Studio runs on 127.0.0.1); the alert carries a timestamp and a 60 s snapshot, never continuous raw EEG. Rolling buffers are deleted by default because EEG can reveal sleep, attention and mood.

Who the model was not trained on

SeizeIT2 is mostly adults with focal epilepsy from five European hospital units; only one centre enrolled children. Performance on infants with spasms, on generalized epilepsies, at home, and on skin and hair types that affect electrode contact is unproven. We report by seizure type and dataset so the limits are visible, including the share of seizures the behind-the-ear EEG never shows at all.

Over-reliance

A family may reduce supervision because "the device is watching". The onboarding wizard and alert wording push against this, and the dashboard shows the device's own miss rate.

Silence must never mean safety

A dead battery is a silent failure. The app warns at 20% and the caregiver app shows "last heartbeat received", so no signal is never mistaken for no seizure. Electrical safety: battery power only, no mains peripherals, written into the README and the wizard.

Autonomy and access

Continuous monitoring can erode autonomy, especially for teenagers: the patient controls start/stop and sees every alert. A local-only mode works with any laptop and a cheap speaker, so the build does not require a modern phone and Wi-Fi.

Rubric · Problem Statement + Presentation · meaningful impact on multiple fronts

Impact is measured in seconds, dollars and data

How fast someone reaches the person seizing. Who can afford to be watched. Whether the neurologist finally sees the real seizure count. The expensive part of monitoring is the specialist reading the signal; ICTRA moves that reading into a model trained on more than 11,000 hours of expert-annotated data, so the cost of watching one more person approaches the cost of three electrodes.

Seconds

Target ≤ 10 s median alert latency

Nocturnal supervision is protective; an alert that wakes a caregiver within seconds addresses the most dangerous moment in epilepsy.

Dollars

Open build, consumer-grade cost

Hardware files, firmware, training code and weights released under an open licence; local-only mode runs on any laptop. 80% of patients live where video-EEG is out of reach.

Data

A seizure diary a neurologist can use

Timestamped, signal-backed events replace diaries that miss over half of seizures, exportable for a 15-minute appointment.
